================================================================================
TASK SCHEDULER AGENT  v3.0.0                          agent id: task_scheduler_agent
================================================================================
The farmer's daily work plan.

The other agents each answer one question - what is the weather, how much
water, which crop, is the soil healthy. This agent answers the question the
farmer actually asks: "what should I do today?"

It takes every specialist agent's output, puts each suggested job on a real
day at a real time, and attaches what to do, what not to do, why it matters
now, and what it costs to wait. It refuses jobs that are unsafe, moves jobs
the weather blocks, and drops jobs the rain has already done for you.

  Stateless   no database, no network, no calls to other agents
  Pure        same input always gives the same plan
  Fast        a full plan takes a few milliseconds; nothing to warm up
  No ML       scheduling is rules, constraints and ordering. A farmer must
              be able to see exactly why a job is on a given day, and a
              model that cannot explain itself would not survive one
              disagreement in the field.


================================================================================
1. HOW IT CONNECTS
================================================================================

   Weather Watcher ──┐
   Irrigation Planner┤
   Soil Health ──────┼─► orchestrator adapters ─► TASK SCHEDULER ─► TaskPlan
   Crop Prediction ──┤        (translate each             │
   Pest / Disease ───┤         agent's own output         ▼
   Market Intel ─────┘         into a block below)   daily work screen
                                                     push alerts
                                                     LLM's spoken answer

Two ways to call it:

  A. API / direct     you build the blocks yourself (section 3)
                      POST /api/task-scheduler/plan

  B. Orchestrator     you pass each agent's RAW output under its own key and
                      the adapters convert it (section 6). A failure in one
                      adapter loses that one agent's contribution, never the
                      whole plan.

Endpoints
  POST /api/task-scheduler/plan      build the plan          -> TaskPlan
  GET  /api/task-scheduler/options   categories, crops, limits
  GET  /api/task-scheduler/health    liveness

Code map
  agent.py      thin wrapper. run() / plan(); as a LangGraph node it never raises
  service.py    the engine - 9 stages, section 7
  playbook.py   what to do / what not to do, by category, crop and situation
  config.py     every threshold, each with its source
  inputs.py     accepts the API shape or raw orchestrator state
  schemas.py    validated input and output

Tests
  python -m AI_Backend.tests.test_task_scheduler
  166 checks, including a sweep of 6,120 farm situations
  (crop x growth stage x weather x resources x days-to-harvest)

================================================================================
4. HOW IT DECIDES
================================================================================

4.1  RAIN IS JUDGED BY AMOUNT, NEVER BY PROBABILITY ALONE
     A 70 % chance of 2 mm replaces nothing. Below 50 % confidence the amount
     is ignored entirely.
       >= 2 mm    washes a fresh spray off the leaf
       >= 10 mm   field too wet for machinery (tillage, sowing, harvest)
       >= 25 mm   leaches fertilizer below the root zone
       Irrigation is cancelled only when the rain covers at least 80 % of the
       planned depth. 40 mm cancels a 30 mm irrigation; 6 mm does not.

4.2  HEAT IS A LIMIT ON THE PERSON, NOT THE CROP
     A 36 C afternoon does not waste the 26 C morning: heat-sensitive work is
     simply booked to finish by 11:00. Only dangerous heat (>= 42 C) blocks a
     day outright.

4.3  PRIORITY
       score = urgency x 0.40 + risk x 0.35 + impact x 0.25   (each 0-10)
       then multiplied by how much the source is trusted:
         weather 1.20 (measured, not modelled)   pest 1.10 (spreads fast)
         irrigation / soil / growth 1.00          crop / market 0.90
       >= 8.0 critical | >= 6.0 high | >= 4.0 medium | >= 2.0 low | else deferred

4.4  ORDER BETWEEN JOBS - hours that must pass, so one job does not undo another
       spray      -> irrigate     24 h   deposits must dry
       irrigate   -> spray        12 h   wet canopy dilutes, wet soil compacts
       fertilize  -> irrigate      6 h   water it in, but let it sit first
       irrigate   -> fertilize    12 h   a saturated surface just runs off
       prepare    -> sow          24 h   let the seedbed settle
       sow        -> irrigate      2 h   germination water
       prune      -> irrigate     24 h   let the wounds close first
     When a job slides for this reason, the farmer is told which job it is
     waiting on.

4.5  DELAY HAS A PRICE - stated on the task, per category per day
       pest 25 % | irrigation 20 % | harvest 15 % | planting 10 %
       market 10 % | fertilizer 8 % | weeding 6 % | tillage 4 % | pruning 3 %
     Past 30 % of value lost, the task says "do this first as soon as
     conditions allow".

4.6  DAY CAPACITY
     At most 8 jobs and 10 working hours per day - a limit on the farmer's
     attention, not on the field. Start times round to the quarter hour and
     never fall before current_timestamp.

4.7  THE TWO RULES IT REFUSES TO BEND
     Everything else is a delay. These are refusals: the job is not placed at
     all and is NOT quietly moved to tomorrow. It appears in refused_tasks
     with the rule, the reason and an alternative.
       * Pre-harvest interval - residue above the legal limit. The produce can
         be rejected at the mandi and is not safe to eat.
       * A bee-toxic spray on a flowering crop - it kills the pollinators that
         set the fruit you are spraying to protect.

5. TASK TYPES AND ADVICE
================================================================================

5.1  The 11 categories and their defaults

CATEGORY           TIME   PEOPLE  EQUIPMENT      DRY AFTER  SPRAY  HEAT-LIMITED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
irrigation          90m     1     irrigation_set     -        -        -
fertilization       60m     1     spreader          6 h       -        -
pest_control        60m     2     sprayer           4 h      yes      yes
soil_preparation   150m     2     tractor            -        -       yes
planting           120m     2     seed_drill         -        -       yes
harvesting         240m     4     harvester          -        -       yes
monitoring          30m     1     -                  -        -        -
pruning            120m     2     secateurs          -        -       yes
weeding            180m     3     -                  -        -       yes
market_action      180m     1     -                  -        -        -
other               60m     1     -                  -        -        -

The source agent's own numbers always win; these are used only when it is
silent.

5.2  Guidance is built in three layers, merged, most specific first
       1. category   true for that work on any farm
       2. crop       what this crop specifically punishes you for
       3. situation   what today's weather, soil or stage adds

     Crops with specific advice (15):
       banana, cabbage, chickpea, cotton, groundnut, maize, mango, mustard,
       onion, potato, rice, soybean, sugarcane, tomato, wheat
     Any other crop gets the general advice; nothing is invented.

     Situations recognised: dangerous heat, hot day, frost tonight, frozen
     ground, gale, wet field, rain coming, saline soil, acid soil, critical
     growth stage, near harvest, low moisture, waterlogged.

     EVERY "do not" carries its reason - a rule a farmer understands is a
     rule they follow. The test suite enforces this.


================================================================================
6. ORCHESTRATOR / LANGGRAPH USE
================================================================================

As a node:   TaskSchedulerAgent()(state)  ->  {"task_plan": {...}}
             Unusable input returns {} and logs a warning - the graph never
             stops because one agent upstream failed.
Directly:    await TaskSchedulerAgent().run(payload)  -> dict
             TaskSchedulerAgent().plan(payload)       -> TaskPlan object

State shape it accepts (farm fields may sit at the top level, and each
agent's RAW output under its own key - the adapters convert them):

  {
    "farm_id": "F1", "field_id": "P1", "current_crop": "groundnut",
    "growth_stage": "flowering", "days_to_harvest": 40,
    "total_area_hectares": 2.0, "current_timestamp": "2026-06-15T05:30:00+05:30",
    "resources": { … },

    "weather_data":        <Weather Watcher output>,
    "soil_data":           <Soil Health output>,
    "irrigation_advice":   <Irrigation Planner output>,
    "crop_recommendation": <Crop Prediction output>,
    "market_insights":     <Market Intelligence output>,
    "pest_disease":        <Pest agent output>
  }

Anything missing gets a safe default rather than an error: no request_id is
generated for you, an unreadable timestamp falls back to now, and an absurd
horizon is clamped.


================================================================================
7. WHAT HAPPENS INSIDE (9 stages)
================================================================================
  1 collect     each agent's output becomes candidate jobs
  2 score       urgency / risk / impact -> one priority          (4.3)
  3 refuse      food-safety and human-safety rules                (4.7)
  4 weather     move each job to a day it can actually be done    (4.1, 4.2)
  5 resources   water, people, equipment the farm really has      (2.2)
  6 conflicts   only genuinely identical jobs are merged - two different
                fertilizers in one day is normal farming, not a conflict
  7 slots       a real start time, honouring the gaps in 4.4
  8 guidance    do / do not / why now / cost of delay             (5.2)
  9 plan        the day, with one headline and one first job

9. WORKED EXAMPLE
================================================================================
REQUEST - groundnut at flowering, dry soil, leaf spot on 30 % of the field,
heavy rain tomorrow, harvest 40 days away.

{
  "request_id": "req-001",
  "planning_horizon_days": 3,
  "farm": { "farm_id": "RJK-1", "field_id": "P1", "current_crop": "groundnut",
            "growth_stage": "flowering", "days_to_harvest": 40,
            "total_area_hectares": 2.0,
            "current_timestamp": "2026-06-15T05:30:00+05:30" },
  "resources": { "labor_units_available": 3, "water_available_liters": 800000 },
  "weather_agent": { "forecast": [
      { "date": "2026-06-15", "temp_min_c": 26, "temp_max_c": 34,
        "rainfall_mm": 0, "rain_probability_percent": 10, "wind_speed_kmh": 8 },
      { "date": "2026-06-16", "temp_min_c": 25, "temp_max_c": 31,
        "rainfall_mm": 40, "rain_probability_percent": 90, "wind_speed_kmh": 22 } ] },
  "irrigation_agent": { "soil_moisture_current_percent": 22, "schedule": [
      { "date": "2026-06-15", "irrigation_required": true, "water_depth_mm": 30,
        "duration_hours": 2, "method": "drip", "timing": "morning" } ] },
  "pest_disease_agent": { "threat_detected": true, "threat_type": "leaf_spot",
      "severity": 7.5, "affected_area_percent": 30, "treatment_type": "fungicide",
      "chemical_name": "mancozeb", "pre_harvest_interval_days": 14 }
}

RESPONSE (abridged)

  headline : "1 urgent job(s) today - start with treat leaf spot with mancozeb."
  do_first : "Treat leaf spot with mancozeb"

  1) Treat leaf spot with mancozeb      15 Jun 06:00-07:00   CRITICAL
     why_now : "It is on about 30% of the field. Left alone, an outbreak this
                size usually doubles within a week."
     do      : "Identify the pest before you spray - the wrong chemical costs
                money and kills the natural enemies that were helping." …
     do_not  : "Do not spray within the pre-harvest interval on the label -
                residue above the limit makes the produce unsellable and unsafe." …
     safety  : "Keep everyone out of the field for 12 hours after spraying.",
               "Do not harvest for 14 day(s) after this spray."

  2) Irrigate - drip (30 mm)            17 Jun 06:00-08:00   CRITICAL
     why_now : "The crop is at flowering - water stress now cannot be made up
                later in the season."
     do      : "Protect the pegging and pod-filling stage above all - water
                stress there is a direct loss of pods."   (groundnut-specific)
     do_not  : "Do not irrigate heavily just before harvest; wet pods at
                lifting are how aflatoxin starts."
     blocked_by   : ["it has to wait after pest control"]
     cost_of_delay: "2 day(s) without water at this stage takes yield you
                     cannot get back. Do this first as soon as conditions allow."

  Why the 17th and not the 16th: water must wait 24 h after the spray (4.4),
  and the 16th brings 40 mm of rain, which covers a 30 mm irrigation (4.1).


================================================================================
10. ERRORS
================================================================================
422   the input could not be read - no agent block, or a field out of range
      (a percentage above 100, a negative area, a malformed "HH:MM")
500   an unexpected failure; the farmer sees a generic message and the detail
      goes to the log, never to the response

As a LangGraph node nothing is raised: unusable state returns {} so one bad
input cannot stop the farm's other answers.

Tuning by environment variable (operations only - agronomic and safety
thresholds are deliberately not overridable):
  FARMXPERT_TS_HORIZON      default planning horizon (3)
  FARMXPERT_TS_MAX_HORIZON  hard maximum (14)
  FARMXPERT_TS_MAX_TASKS    jobs per day (8)
  FARMXPERT_TS_MAX_HOURS    working hours per day (10)
  FARMXPERT_TS_WORK_START   default day start ("06:00")
  FARMXPERT_TS_WORK_END     default day end ("18:00")
