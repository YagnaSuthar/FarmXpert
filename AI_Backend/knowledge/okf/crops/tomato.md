# Tomato

Even moisture prevents blossom-end rot; an open canopy prevents blight.

Type: Vegetable. Season: Kharif, rabi and summer.

- Salt tolerance is ECe, measured on a saturated paste extract in a laboratory (FAO-29, Maas-Hoffman). A field meter reading a 1:2 soil-water sample gives a different, lower number - the two must not be compared directly.
- The depletion fraction p is the share of the soil's available water this crop can use before it starts to suffer. At p = 0.4, irrigate once about 40 percent of the available water in the root zone has been used.

## Water

- Blossom-end rot is almost always a watering fault, not a calcium shortage. Buying calcium spray will not fix an irregular irrigation schedule.

## Nutrition

- Calcium availability depends on steady water. Liming an acid soil helps; erratic irrigation undoes it.

## Soil

- Well-drained loam, pH 6.0-7.0. It is moderately sensitive to salt.

## Problems to watch

- Fruit borer - scout and hand-pick early; it is far cheaper than a late spray programme.

## Harvest

- Pick at breaker stage for distant markets, ripe for local sale.

## Do and do not

The rules the task scheduler attaches to this crop's jobs.

### Irrigation

- Do not: Do not water irregularly during fruiting - uneven moisture with low calcium causes blossom-end rot.

### Pest control

- Do: Stake and prune for airflow; a dense canopy is what keeps leaves wet long enough for blight.

## Soil notes

- Blossom-end rot is calcium failing to reach the fruit, usually from irregular watering - keep soil moisture even.
- Boron 1-2 kg/ha on B-poor soils improves fruit set.
