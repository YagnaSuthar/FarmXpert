# Guar (cluster bean)

A hardy rainfed legume: it fails from too much water far more often than too little.

Type: Pulse legume. Season: Kharif (June-July sowing), mostly rainfed.

- Salt tolerance is ECe, measured on a saturated paste extract in a laboratory (FAO-29, Maas-Hoffman). A field meter reading a 1:2 soil-water sample gives a different, lower number - the two must not be compared directly.
- The depletion fraction p is the share of the soil's available water this crop can use before it starts to suffer. At p = 0.55, irrigate once about 55 percent of the available water in the root zone has been used.

## Water

- The first sign of trouble is wilting in a wet field, not a dry one - that is root rot, and irrigating again finishes the crop.

## Nutrition

- A legume - inoculate the seed and keep nitrogen minimal.

## Soil

- Sandy loam suits it. It tolerates heat and moderate salinity better than most pulses.

## Problems to watch

- Bacterial blight in wet spells; use clean seed and avoid overhead irrigation.
- Jassids in dry heat.

## Harvest

- For gum, harvest when pods dry on the plant; for vegetable use, pick tender.

## Do and do not

The rules the task scheduler attaches to this crop's jobs.

### Fertilization

- Do: Inoculate the seed; guar fixes its own nitrogen.
- Do not: Do not apply heavy nitrogen - it grows haulm instead of pods and delays maturity.

### Irrigation

- Do: Guar is grown rainfed in most of Gujarat; one irrigation at flowering is usually all it needs.
- Do not: Do not let water stand, even briefly - guar collapses from root rot far more often than from drought.

## Soil notes

- Responds strongly to phosphorus (40 kg P2O5/ha) and Rhizobium inoculation; needs little nitrogen.
