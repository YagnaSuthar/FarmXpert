# Wheat

The crown root irrigation about 21 days after sowing sets tiller number and the season's yield.

Type: Cereal. Season: Rabi (November-December sowing).

- Salt tolerance is ECe, measured on a saturated paste extract in a laboratory (FAO-29, Maas-Hoffman). A field meter reading a 1:2 soil-water sample gives a different, lower number - the two must not be compared directly.
- The depletion fraction p is the share of the soil's available water this crop can use before it starts to suffer. At p = 0.55, irrigate once about 55 percent of the available water in the root zone has been used.

## Water

- Flowering and grain filling are the other two stages that cannot be missed.

## Soil

- Prefers well-drained loam. Waterlogging for even a day at tillering shows up as yellowing and lost tillers.

## Problems to watch

- Yellow rust in cool humid weather - scout the lower leaves from tillering.
- Heavy irrigation with wind during grain filling lays the crop flat and costs both yield and harvest speed.

## Harvest

- Physiological maturity is when filling stops, but the grain is still about 30 percent moisture - too wet to combine or store. Wait until it hardens to roughly 14 percent, or harvest wetter and dry it down.
- Do not leave a ripe crop standing. Every extra day risks lodging, rain, hail and shrivelled grain, and a lodged crop is far slower to cut.

## Do and do not

The rules the task scheduler attaches to this crop's jobs.

### Irrigation

- Do: Never miss the crown-root-initiation irrigation about 21 days after sowing - it sets the number of tillers.
- Do not: Do not irrigate heavily when the grain is filling and wind is expected; a wet, top-heavy crop lodges.

## Soil notes

- Split nitrogen: half at sowing, the rest at crown-root initiation (~21 days) and tillering.
- Zinc sulphate 25 kg/ha on zinc-deficient soils.
