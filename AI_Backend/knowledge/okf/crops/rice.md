# Rice (paddy)

Water depth is the management tool: keep a shallow layer, and drain at maturity.

Type: Cereal. Season: Kharif (transplanted June-July).

- Salt tolerance is ECe, measured on a saturated paste extract in a laboratory (FAO-29, Maas-Hoffman). A field meter reading a 1:2 soil-water sample gives a different, lower number - the two must not be compared directly.
- The depletion fraction p is the share of the soil's available water this crop can use before it starts to suffer. At p = 0.2, irrigate once about 20 percent of the available water in the root zone has been used.

## Water

- Drain at physiological maturity so the field is firm for harvest and the grain dries evenly.

## Nutrition

- Urea broadcast into deep standing water is largely lost to the water itself rather than taken up by the crop.

## Soil

- Puddled clay holds water best. On light soil the water cost is much higher.

## Problems to watch

- Blast and bacterial leaf blight in humid weather; avoid excess nitrogen, which makes both worse.
- Stem borer - watch for dead hearts, act on trap counts rather than the calendar.

## Harvest

- Harvest at about 20 percent grain moisture and dry down; over-dry grain breaks during milling.

## Do and do not

The rules the task scheduler attaches to this crop's jobs.

### Fertilization

- Do not: Do not top-dress urea into deep standing water; drain to a thin film, apply, then re-flood.

### Irrigation

- Do: Keep 5 cm standing water from tillering to grain filling, and drain at physiological maturity.
- Do not: Do not let the field dry and crack during flowering - the panicle sets fewer grains and does not recover.

## Soil notes

- Zinc deficiency (khaira) is common in flooded rice - zinc sulphate 25 kg/ha at puddling.
- Place urea in the reduced layer or use deep placement to cut losses.
